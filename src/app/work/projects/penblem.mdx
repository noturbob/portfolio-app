## Overview
Penblem turns an Apple Pencil (non-Pro) into a pressure-sensitive stylus on a non-touch Linux laptop. Pressure and taps come from the Pencil over BLE, absolute X/Y comes from the laptop's own touchpad, and both are fused into a virtual pen tablet exposed through `/dev/uinput`.
## Key Features
- **BLE probing**: `penblem probe` dumps the Pencil's BLE characteristics and live notification bytes to discover the (non-public) pressure and tap UUIDs.
- **Sensor fusion**: `penblem run` grabs the touchpad and emits a virtual "penblem Apple Pencil" tablet device.
- **Stale-pressure guard**: Pressure only counts if a BLE packet arrived within a configurable window, so an idle Pencil can't leave a stuck pressure value that a bare finger would inherit.
- **One-shot setup**: A setup script installs BlueZ and libinput tooling, loads `uinput` and installs udev rules.
## Technologies Used
- **Rust**, **BlueZ / Bluetooth LE**, **Linux uinput and evdev**.
## Challenges and Learnings
Testing showed the laptop's capacitive touchpad doesn't sense the Pencil's bare plastic tip at all, so the touchpad half needs a conductive nib — a hardware constraint that shaped the design.
